# AgentNine logo and favicon brief

## Design direction

AgentNine is a source-linked directory for discovering and comparing AI-agent projects. Its listings help people inspect project repositories, setup requirements, and documented access before deciding what to run. It is not a certification, safety guarantee, endorsement, or claim of affiliation with listed projects.

The logo should feel authored, editorial, and exact: a memorable identifier with a strong silhouette, not an illustration of “AI” or a diagram explaining every feature. “Nine” is the name, not a claim that the directory contains nine projects.

**Color is a requirement, not an invitation to invent palettes:**

- Brand red: `#DD0200`. Use it as the solid field behind the symbol in the logo mark and favicon.
- Light mode: the symbol is ink `#17181A` on the red field; the adjacent wordmark is ink on a light canvas.
- Dark mode: the symbol is warm white `#FFF8F6` on the same red field; the adjacent wordmark is warm white on a dark canvas.
- Show both theme versions. Do not substitute a dark tile, add a gradient, or introduce accent colors.

The current `app/icon.svg` is an existing reference only. Do not trace it or alter it.

## Copyable prompt for Claude

```text
Create a first-round logo and favicon exploration for AgentNine. I need exactly ten distinct creative directions to compare, with a logo and matching favicon for each. Treat this as identity design, not an exercise in generating ten random icons.

## Understand the product

AgentNine is a source-linked directory for discovering and comparing AI-agent projects. A listing helps people inspect upstream repositories, setup requirements, and documented system/provider access before deciding what to run. AgentNine is not a security certification, safety guarantee, endorsement, or affiliation with the listed projects. The name “AgentNine” does not mean there are exactly nine listings.

The identity should be distinctive, considered, editorial, and clear at a glance. It should belong to this specific product, rather than looking like a generic AI startup, developer tool, or logo template.

## Fixed color direction

Use the existing AgentNine red as the logo symbol's solid background field: #DD0200.

Make both required theme versions:
- Light mode: ink #17181A symbol on the red field; adjacent ink wordmark on a light canvas.
- Dark mode: warm white #FFF8F6 symbol on the same red field; adjacent warm-white wordmark on a dark canvas.

The favicon follows this same rule: red field, ink mark for light mode, warm-white mark for dark mode. In lockups, put the red field behind the symbol, with the wordmark beside it on the theme's light or dark canvas. Keep the mark and lettering legible. The red field is part of the identity, not a generic rounded app tile added behind an unrelated icon. Do not use a dark field in place of the red field. Do not add gradients, glows, shadows, or other accent colors. You may show the mark in plain black or white as a one-color craft check, but the presented theme versions must follow the red-field rule above.

## Design the concepts

First inspect the repository's docs/design/DESIGN_RULES.md, docs/product/PROJECT_REFERENCE.md, app/globals.css, and app/icon.svg if available. Use them to understand the existing product and visual context. The current icon is not a template: do not trace it, lightly modify it, or assume it is approved.

Develop exactly these ten starting territories. Each must become an original logo symbol and a simplified favicon derived from that symbol. These are strategic prompts, not instructions to draw literal clip art. Interpret each with a specific, memorable form. If a territory cannot be made ownable, replace it and explain why, rather than delivering a weak or generic mark.

1. **The source cut**: A custom, compact mark built around one precise interruption or incision. The idea is that AgentNine lets people inspect the source behind a project. Make the cut part of the silhouette, not a magnifying-glass handle, link, or external arrow.
2. **The index rhythm**: A typographic or abstract mark using a small number of deliberately unequal strokes, gaps, or alignments, like a distinctive editorial index. It should suggest curation without depicting a bulleted list or implying a fixed number of agents.
3. **The open record**: A single open-sided form with one carefully placed inner counter or notch. It should suggest an entry that can be inspected, while avoiding a browser-window outline, document icon, or generic rounded-square app tile.
4. **The comparison seam**: Two unequal forms meeting at one exact seam, with their relationship doing the work. It should hint at comparing projects without becoming a Venn diagram, chain link, pair of circles, or mirrored twin.
5. **The AgentNine ligature**: An original A/N typographic construction where a shared stroke, counter, or overlap creates the identity. Both letters should remain legible at useful sizes; do not settle for stock initials inside a badge.
6. **The open nine**: A custom numeral 9 with an unusual open counter or terminal that gives it a distinctive silhouette. It must read as a brand letterform, not a count of nine listings, a rating, or a verification seal. Include the risk that a 9 may be misread or overemphasize the name.
7. **The source-to-setup turn**: One continuous, restrained path that changes direction once, from a source-like origin to a distinct endpoint. Make the geometry feel intentional and ownable, not a generic Git branch, flowchart, circuit, or navigation arrow.
8. **The editorial colophon**: A compact typographic stamp or publisher-like device with one unexpected proportion or negative-space detail. It should convey a carefully maintained directory, not authority, certification, or a vintage seal.
9. **The selected counterform**: A bold, simple outer shape whose identity is created by one off-center void or cutout. Make the void memorable and meaningful, but do not use a checkmark, eye, shield, or star.
10. **The wordmark's own device**: A wordmark-first direction in which one custom detail in “AgentNine” becomes the favicon mark. The detail must come from the actual letterforms, not a separately pasted-on stock symbol. State whether the letters are custom-drawn or set in a named typeface.

For each concept, first write: name, one-sentence idea, what makes its silhouette different from the other nine, and one honest risk. Then build it. The ten concepts must vary in mark type and geometry, not just color, font, angle, or small details. Do not force a literal number nine or A/N construction into every option.

Avoid generic AI shorthand such as robots, brains, circuit boards, neural nodes, sparkles, generic shields, and generic cubes. Do not use visual language that suggests AgentNine verifies or certifies project safety. Do not trace or closely imitate any existing logo. Keep the mark simple enough to recognize at favicon scale, with one clear gesture rather than stacked metaphors or decorative detail.

## Deliverables

Save all work as unapproved drafts in docs/design/logo-concepts/round-01/. Do not edit or replace app/icon.svg, app/favicon.ico, or any other live site asset, code, or styling. Do not install packages or add dependencies.

For each of the ten concepts, provide separate, clearly named light and dark files:
- symbol-light.svg and symbol-dark.svg: standalone mark on the red field, using ink for light mode and warm white for dark mode;
- lockup-light.svg and lockup-dark.svg: symbol paired with the exact name “AgentNine”, using the corresponding foreground color;
- favicon-light.svg and favicon-dark.svg: simplified small-size companion marks following the same red-field and foreground-color rule;
- notes.md: concept name, one-sentence idea, form rationale, any typeface and licensing assumption, and one honest weakness or risk.

Use numbered folders, 01 through 10, with a short concept name. Include a README.md that explains the draft-only status and lists every deliverable. Keep SVGs clean, editable, and self-contained. Do not embed raster images. If the lockup uses live text, say so and identify the font; do not call it custom lettering unless the letterforms are actually drawn.

Create two straightforward PNG comparison sheets, concepts 01–05 and 06–10. Each should show the light and dark lockups, plus each theme's favicon at actual 64 px, 32 px, and 16 px on red fields. Keep the layout quiet and spacious so the marks, not presentation effects, hold attention. Label every concept with its name and one-sentence rationale.

## Inspect before reporting

Render the SVGs and sheets if a renderer is available. Actually inspect the results, including all 16 px favicon renders and both theme versions. Fix marks whose counters or gaps close, whose silhouette becomes generic, or whose light/dark treatment loses legibility. Do not claim a size or theme was tested unless you rendered and inspected it. If rendering is unavailable, say exactly which checks could not be performed.

Report the ten concept names, point out how their ideas and silhouettes differ, and recommend the strongest three with a concise reason and trade-off for each. These are design judgments, not trademark research. Do not claim legal availability or trademark clearance.

Stop after presenting the ten concepts and comparison sheets. Wait for me to choose or request revisions before making a final logo kit or applying any design to the live website.
```
